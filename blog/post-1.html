Routing my first 4-layer PCB

It felt like learning a new language at the same time as learning a new craft. The board had to be functional, testable, and compact enough to fit the design goals.

What I was trying to solve

My first real routing challenge was not just arranging components on a board. It was reducing the amount of noise, simplifying power delivery, and keeping the layout readable enough to debug later.

What changed

I started with a much simpler two-layer idea, then realized the power and signal paths were getting crowded. Adding a second internal plane changed the whole workflow: I could separate power and ground more deliberately and keep the routing paths cleaner.

Lessons I kept

  • Route the critical high-speed paths first, then fill around them.
  • Put test points near the parts that need measurement.
  • Don’t combine power traces and signal traces if they share the same return path.
  • Give the board enough room for debug access while it is still in prototype form.

Next time

I want to do a tighter review of the power tree before placement, then run a more complete ground strategy before generating the final Gerbers. The trick is not just making it work — it is making it easier to troubleshoot once it does.
